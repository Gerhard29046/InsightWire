## 1. Introduction

This is the manual required by **section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 ("PAIA")**, prepared by **[Operator Name]** ("the private body"), the operator of InsightWire.

PAIA gives effect to the constitutional right of access to any information held by the State, and to any information held by another person that is required for the exercise or protection of any rights. Section 50(1) sets the test for a request to a **private body**: a requester must be given access to a record if the record is **required for the exercise or protection of any rights**, the procedural requirements have been complied with, and access is not refused on a ground in Chapter 4 of Part 3 of the Act.

**Every private body must have a PAIA manual.** The exemption that previously relieved certain private bodies of this obligation lapsed on 31 December 2021; from 1 January 2022 no private body is exempt. Section 51(2) requires the manual to be updated on a regular basis, and section 51(3) requires it to be made available on the body's website, at its principal place of business, to any person on request against a reasonable fee, and to the Information Regulator.

## 7. How to Request Access to a Record — sections 53 and 50

### Step 1 — Complete the prescribed form

Submit a request on **Form 2 (Request for Access to Record of Private Body)**, prescribed by the PAIA Regulations. The form is obtainable from the Information Regulator's website and will be supplied by us on request.

### Step 2 — Provide sufficient particulars

Section 53(2) requires the request to:

- provide sufficient particulars to enable the Information Officer to identify the record and to identify you as the requester;
- indicate the form of access you require;
- specify a postal address or email address in the Republic;
- **identify the right you are seeking to exercise or protect, and explain why the requested record is required for the exercise or protection of that right** — this is the section 50(1)(a) threshold and a request that does not address it will be refused;
- state if you wish to be informed of the decision in any manner other than in writing, and give the necessary particulars; and
- if you are acting on behalf of another person, state that capacity and provide proof of it to the Information Officer's satisfaction.

### Step 3 — Send it to us

Email **[Contact Email]**, addressed to the Information Officer, or deliver it to **[Jurisdiction/Registered Address]**.

### Step 4 — Pay the prescribed request fee

We will notify you of the prescribed request fee. It must be paid before the request is processed, and it is not refundable. We will then process the request and, where access is granted, notify you of any further access fee (and, where applicable, a deposit) before the record is released.

> **Fee amounts must be verified.** The PAIA Regulations prescribe the fee schedule for private bodies, including the request fee, per-page reproduction fees, fees for other formats, search time, postage and deposits. The commonly cited request fee for a private body is **R140.00**, but the operator must confirm the current prescribed amounts from the Regulations then in force before publishing this manual, and must republish this section whenever the Regulations change. A personal requester seeking their own personal information is exempt from the request fee.

### Step 5 — Our decision

The Information Officer must decide and notify you **within 30 days** of receiving the request (PAIA section 56), and must give reasons if access is refused. That period may be extended in the circumstances permitted by section 57, in which case we will tell you why.

Where the record contains information about a third party, we must follow the third-party notification and representation procedure in sections 71 to 73 before deciding.

## 8. Grounds on Which Access May or Must Be Refused

Chapter 4 of Part 3 of PAIA sets out the grounds. In summary, access may or must be refused where disclosure would involve:

| Section | Ground |
| --- | --- |
| 63 | Unreasonable disclosure of personal information about a third party, including a deceased person |
| 64 | Commercial information of a third party — trade secrets, financial/commercial/scientific/technical information likely to cause harm, or information supplied in confidence |
| 65 | Breach of a duty of confidence owed to a third party |
| 66 | Endangerment of the life or physical safety of an individual, or prejudice to the security of property or a means of transport |
| 67 | Prejudice to legal proceedings in respect of which legal professional privilege could be claimed |
| 68 | Harm to the commercial or financial interests of the private body, or to its position in negotiations |
| 69 | Prejudice to research information of the private body or a third party |
| 70 | **Mandatory disclosure in the public interest**, which overrides most of the grounds above where disclosure would reveal a substantial contravention of the law or an imminent and serious public safety or environmental risk, and the public interest in disclosure clearly outweighs the harm |

## 9. If We Refuse — Remedies

There is no internal appeal against a decision of a private body. If your request is refused, or deemed refused, you may:

- **complain to the Information Regulator**, at PAIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za; and/or
- **apply to a court** for appropriate relief under section 78 of PAIA.
