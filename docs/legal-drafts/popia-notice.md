## 3. The Nature of Our Processing

InsightWire aggregates publicly available news, government, institutional and multilateral event information from roughly eighteen public feeds and APIs, and presents it through research interfaces. For each event we store a headline, a short summary, the source name, the original source URL, a publication timestamp and an ingestion timestamp. **We never reproduce full source articles**; we link to them.

The personal information we process falls into three narrow groups:

1. **Personal information contained in public source material** — overwhelmingly the names and roles of public officials, institutional representatives and organisations reported on by public sources, plus entities extracted from that text.
2. **Technical request information** — IP address, user-agent and requested path, handled transiently by our hosting and edge infrastructure to route, serve and protect requests.
3. **Correspondence** — what you send us when you contact us.

We do **not** operate user accounts, set cookies, run analytics or advertising technology, process payments, conduct direct marketing, or sell personal information.

## 4. Section 7 — Journalistic Purposes

POPIA section 7 excludes from the Act's application the processing of personal information solely for the purpose of journalistic, literary or artistic expression, to the extent that the exclusion is necessary to reconcile, as a matter of public interest, the right to privacy with the right to freedom of expression.

InsightWire is a journalism and research tool, and much of what it does sits close to the centre of that provision. **We do not rely on section 7 as a blanket exemption.** We design and operate as though POPIA applies in full, because doing so produces a better and more defensible system, and because the scope of section 7 for a platform of this kind has not been authoritatively settled. Reviewing counsel should form their own view.

## 5. The Eight Conditions for Lawful Processing

POPIA establishes eight conditions: Accountability (s 8), Processing Limitation (ss 9–12), Purpose Specification (ss 13–14), Further Processing Limitation (s 15), Information Quality (s 16), Openness (ss 17–18), Security Safeguards (ss 19–22), and Data Subject Participation (ss 23–25). Here is how each maps onto what the Platform actually does.

### Condition 1 — Accountability (section 8)

We accept responsibility for compliance at the point where we determine the purpose and means of processing, and throughout the processing. In practice this means: a named Information Officer; a documented processor list; policies that describe the system as it really is rather than as we would like it to look; and a commitment to correct these documents when the system changes.

### Condition 2 — Processing Limitation (sections 9–12)

**Lawfulness and minimality (s 9, s 10).** We process the minimum that the Service requires. The clearest evidence of this is what we chose not to build: no accounts, no cookies, no trackers, no profiling, no enrichment of individuals.

**Justification (s 11).** We rely principally on section 11(1)(f) — processing necessary for pursuing the legitimate interests of the responsible party and of the users of a public-interest research and journalism service. For correspondence we rely on consent and, where applicable, legal obligation under section 11(1)(a) and (b).

**Collection directly from the data subject (s 12).** We do not collect from data subjects directly. We collect from published public sources. Section 12(2) permits collection from another source where the information is contained in a public record or has deliberately been made public by the data subject, and where collection from another source would not prejudice a legitimate interest of the data subject. Government and institutional publications are the paradigm case.

**Objection (s 11(3)).** A data subject may object to processing on reasonable grounds relating to their particular situation. We accept and act on such objections through the **Removal and Correction Request Policy**.

### Condition 3 — Purpose Specification (sections 13–14)

**Specific purpose (s 13).** Personal information is processed for one purpose: to operate an informational and research tool that makes publicly reported events discoverable, navigable and verifiable against their original sources. It is not processed for advertising, resale, individual scoring, or any form of surveillance.

**Retention (s 14).** Section 14(1) requires that records not be kept longer than necessary, and section 14(2) permits longer retention for historical, statistical or research purposes where appropriate safeguards are established. The Platform's event archive is retained on that research basis — a research record that silently disappears is not a research record. Retention is governed by the **Data Retention Policy** and is always subject to correction, restriction and removal on request.

### Condition 4 — Further Processing Limitation (section 15)

Further processing must be compatible with the original purpose. Everything the Platform does downstream of ingestion — summarising, categorising, extracting entities, deduplicating, mapping, timelining — serves the same purpose of making public information navigable. We do not repurpose ingested data for marketing, profiling, resale or any unrelated activity. Section 15(3)(b) expressly treats further processing as compatible where the information is publicly available or has been made public by the data subject.

### Condition 5 — Information Quality (section 16)

We must take reasonably practicable steps to ensure information is complete, accurate, not misleading and updated where necessary, having regard to the purpose.

Our honest position: **we are a mirror of our sources, and we are not the author of the underlying facts.** What we can and do commit to is:

- preserving provenance — source name, original URL, publication time and ingestion time — so any item can be checked at origin;
- publishing a standing warning that information may be incomplete, delayed, inaccurate or subsequently corrected, and that users must verify independently (see the **Research and Information Disclaimer**);
- accepting and acting on correction requests; and
- correcting or removing an item where a source correction or a substantiated complaint shows it to be wrong.

### Condition 6 — Openness (sections 17–18)

**Documentation (s 17).** We maintain the documentation required by section 14 or 51 of PAIA — see the **PAIA Manual**.

**Notification (s 18).** This notice, read with the Privacy Policy, is our section 18 notification: it states who we are, what we collect, from where, why, on what basis, who receives it, whether transfers cross borders, and what rights exist.

Because we collect from public sources rather than from data subjects, individual notification is generally not reasonably practicable, and section 18(4)(f) contemplates non-compliance with individual notification where the information comes from a public record, has deliberately been made public, or where compliance would prejudice a lawful purpose of collection. Publishing this notice openly is how we discharge the spirit of the condition.

### Condition 7 — Security Safeguards (sections 19–22)

**Integrity and confidentiality (s 19).** We maintain appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures: all privileged credentials are server-side only and never exposed to the browser; the browser cannot reach the database directly and must go through our API; cross-origin access is constrained; traffic is served over HTTPS; and the collection surface is deliberately minimal.

**Operators (ss 20–21).** Our operators are Supabase (database), Cloudflare (application backend and edge), and Google (Gemini API, for on-demand brief generation and entity extraction). Section 21 requires a written contract obliging the operator to maintain the security measures in section 19. **Executing and retaining those written agreements with each provider is an outstanding pre-launch item.**

**Security compromises (s 22).** Where there are reasonable grounds to believe personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, we will notify the Information Regulator and the affected data subjects as soon as reasonably possible after discovering the compromise, subject to the legitimate needs of law enforcement and to measures reasonably needed to determine the scope of the compromise and restore system integrity. The Regulator prescribes a security compromise notification form, which we will use.

We claim no certification, audit or penetration test, because none has been performed.

### Condition 8 — Data Subject Participation (sections 23–25)

You may request confirmation of whether we hold personal information about you, and a description of it (section 23); and you may request correction or deletion of information that is inaccurate, irrelevant, excessive, out of date, incomplete, misleading or unlawfully obtained, or destruction of information we are no longer authorised to retain (section 24). Section 25 governs the manner of access, which is aligned with PAIA.

**Practical reality:** because we hold no account for you, in most cases we will hold nothing that identifies you, and that will be our honest answer. Where your personal information appears in ingested public source material, the **Removal and Correction Request Policy** is the correct route and will produce a substantive outcome.

## 6. Special Personal Information and Children

Section 26 prohibits processing special personal information — religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health or sex life, biometric information, and criminal behaviour or alleged offences — unless a section 27 ground applies.

We do not deliberately collect special personal information. Public reporting nonetheless contains it. We therefore build no feature that isolates, indexes, filters or targets individuals by special category; we do not infer or enrich special-category attributes; and we treat removal requests involving special-category information as high priority.

Section 34 prohibits processing the personal information of children except as permitted by section 35. We apply heightened restraint to any material concerning minors, as set out in the **Country and Jurisdiction Data Policy**.

## 7. Direct Marketing (section 69)

Section 69 restricts unsolicited electronic direct marketing. **We do not conduct direct marketing of any kind.** We operate no mailing list and send no marketing communications. If that ever changes, this notice will be updated first, and the section 69 opt-in requirements will be implemented before any message is sent.

## 8. Cross-Border Transfers (section 72)

Section 72 prohibits transferring personal information about a data subject to a third party in a foreign country unless a listed ground applies — principally that the recipient is subject to a law, binding corporate rules or a binding agreement providing an adequate level of protection with principles substantially similar to POPIA's conditions and equivalent onward-transfer restrictions; or the data subject consents; or the transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract; or it is for the data subject's benefit where consent is not reasonably practicable.

Our infrastructure providers are international, so once deployed, processing will or may occur outside the Republic. We rely on the providers' data-processing agreements and standard contractual clauses, **which the operator must execute before production launch**, and we minimise transfer volume primarily by not collecting personal information in the first place.
