## 1. Purpose

InsightWire aggregates publicly available information. Sometimes that information is wrong, unlawful, unfairly intrusive, infringing, or harmful. When it is, there must be a real way to get it fixed — not a disclaimer, and not silence.

This policy sets out the categories of request we accept, how to make one, how we assess it, what outcomes are possible, and what to do if you disagree with our decision. It applies to content displayed on the Platform, including ingested event records, derived metadata such as categories and extracted entities, and automatically generated summaries and briefs.

The engineering implementation of this workflow — the intake form, the case record, the audit trail — is being built separately. **This document states the policy that implementation must serve.**

## 2. What We Can and Cannot Do

**We can:** correct our own record; correct or remove a summary, category, entity, geographic placement, deduplication linkage or link; restrict an item so it no longer appears; remove an item entirely; stop ingesting a source; and annotate an item with a correction note.

**We cannot:** change what a source published. If the underlying article is wrong, the authoritative fix is at the source, and we will tell you so and, where appropriate, help you identify the right contact. Removing our copy does not remove the original.

## 3. Request Categories

We recognise nine categories. If you are unsure which applies, choose the closest — we will re-categorise if needed.

### 3.1 Inaccurate information
The item states something factually wrong: an incorrect fact, date, place, number, quotation, or attribution of a statement or role to the wrong person or organisation. Includes errors introduced by our own summarisation, classification or entity extraction.

### 3.2 Privacy violation
The item discloses information about a person in a way that unreasonably intrudes on their privacy, even if the information is technically public — for example a private individual's involvement in a matter of no public interest, or an old item that continues to cause harm disproportionate to any remaining public interest.

### 3.3 Personal information
A request under data protection law: access, correction, objection, restriction, deletion or erasure of personal information, under sections 5, 11(3), 23 and 24 of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 ("POPIA"), or by analogy under GDPR Articles 15 to 22. Includes requests concerning special personal information under POPIA section 26 (religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health or sex life, biometric information, criminal behaviour) and personal information of children under section 34.

### 3.4 Copyright complaint
Material is used in a way that infringes copyright or is displayed contrary to the rights holder's wishes — an over-long summary, missing or wrong attribution, a broken or absent source link, or a request to cease ingesting a feed. See the **Copyright and Content Policy** for what to include.

### 3.5 Unlawful content
The content is unlawful under applicable law — defamatory, in contempt of court, in breach of a court order or reporting restriction, hate speech, incitement, in breach of sanctions or export controls, or otherwise prohibited. Please identify the law or order you rely on.

### 3.6 Harmful content
Content that is not necessarily unlawful but creates a real risk of harm — content that could facilitate violence, exposes a person to danger, identifies a protected person, victim or witness, or discloses information whose publication creates a safety risk.

### 3.7 Source correction
Our record of the source is wrong: wrong source name, wrong or dead original link, wrong publication or ingestion timestamp, or an item attributed to a publisher that did not publish it. Also used where a source has published a correction, update or retraction that we have not reflected.

### 3.8 Impersonation
Content, an entity record, or a source misrepresents a person or organisation — an entity record conflating two people, a statement attributed to someone who did not make it, or a source purporting to be an institution it is not.

### 3.9 Other legal complaint
Anything legal that does not fit above — regulatory notices, contractual restrictions, statutory reporting bans, or a legal demand from a person or authority.

## 4. How to Submit a Request

Email **[Contact Email]** with the subject line **"Removal / correction request — [category]"**.

Please include:

1. **The category** from section 3.
2. **The item(s)** — the URL on the Platform, or the headline, source and date, or the entity or timeline concerned. Be specific; a request we cannot locate cannot be actioned.
3. **What is wrong** — plainly and specifically.
4. **What you want** — correction, annotation, restriction, removal, or cessation of source ingestion.
5. **Why** — the factual or legal basis. For a legal complaint, identify the law, order or right relied on.
6. **Your relationship to the matter** — the person concerned, their representative, the rights holder, the publisher, an authority, or a member of the public.
7. **Your contact details** for our response.
8. **Supporting material** — a link to the authoritative source, a correction notice, a court order, or proof of authority to act for another person.

We may ask for more information, or for proof of identity or authority where a request concerns someone's personal information. We ask for the minimum needed, and we do not retain identity documents longer than necessary to verify the request.

**Requests are accepted from anyone.** You do not need to be the person affected, and you do not need a lawyer.

## 5. The Workflow: Report → Review → Outcome

### Stage 1 — Report
The request is received and recorded as a case with a reference, a timestamp, the category, the items concerned, the requested outcome, and the material supplied. **We acknowledge receipt**, normally within **[X] business days**, and give you the reference.

Where a request discloses an **imminent risk to a person's safety**, we may restrict the item immediately, before review. Safety first, assessment second.

### Stage 2 — Review
We assess the request against:

- **the facts** — what the source actually published, and what our record says;
- **the law** — POPIA, PAIA, the Copyright Act 98 of 1978, the Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020, defamation and contempt principles, and any other applicable law or order;
- **the public interest** — the value of the information to journalism, research, accountability and public understanding;
- **the harm** — the seriousness, likelihood and reversibility of the harm alleged, and whether it falls on a public figure acting publicly or on a private individual;
- **proportionality** — whether a lesser step than removal would resolve it; and
- **our own policies**, particularly the **Country and Jurisdiction Data Policy**, which sets the default in favour of institutional and public-source information and against unnecessary exposure of private individuals.

Where the item concerns a third party whose interests would be affected by the outcome, we may seek their input before deciding.

**Presumptions we apply.** Requests concerning **minors**, **special personal information**, **victims and witnesses**, and **imminent safety risks** are prioritised and are resolved in favour of restraint where the assessment is close. Requests to remove accurate, lawful reporting about a public official acting in an official capacity face a high bar, because that is exactly the material a public-interest research tool exists to preserve.

We aim to complete review within **30 days**, aligning with the standard decision period under section 56 of PAIA. Complex or contested matters may take longer, and we will tell you if so and why.

### Stage 3 — Outcome
One or more of:

| Outcome | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Correct** | We amend the record — the fact, summary, category, entity, geography, source name, link or timestamp — and, where the original was materially wrong, add a correction note. |
| **Annotate** | We leave the item but attach context: a correction notice, a note that the source has retracted, or a note that the matter is disputed. |
| **Restrict** | The item is withheld from public display but retained internally, so the decision is auditable and reversible. Used where removal is likely correct but assessment is incomplete, or where a safety risk requires immediate action. |
| **Remove** | The item is removed from the Platform. Where the request is a valid deletion request under POPIA section 24 or its GDPR analogue, deletion extends to the underlying record. |
| **De-ingest source** | We stop ingesting the source entirely, where the problem is systemic or the publisher has withdrawn permission. |
| **Restore** | A previously restricted or removed item is reinstated because the restriction was wrong, the complaint was withdrawn, or the basis fell away. |
| **Decline** | We take no action. We will tell you why, and tell you what you can do next. |

**Every outcome is communicated in writing, with reasons.** We do not resolve requests silently.

### Stage 4 — Record
Each case is recorded: request, category, assessment, decision, reasons, action taken, and who decided. This record exists so that decisions are consistent, reversible, and defensible — and so that we can be held to this policy.

## 6. If You Disagree

**Ask us to reconsider.** Reply to the case reference with anything you think we missed. Reconsideration is free and there is no time limit. **Where we conclude we got it wrong, we reverse it** — including restoring content we should not have removed.

**Escalate externally.** You may also:

- complain to the **Information Regulator (South Africa)** about handling of personal information — POPIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za — or about a refused records request — PAIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za. Woodmead North Office Park, 54 Maxwell Drive, Woodmead, Johannesburg, 2191. Telephone 010 023 5200 · Toll free 0800 017 160. https://inforegulator.org.za
- complain to your **EU or UK supervisory authority** if you are in those jurisdictions;
- pursue **court remedies**, including under section 78 of PAIA or section 99 of POPIA.

Nothing in this policy limits your legal rights or requires you to use this process first.

## 7. Abuse of This Process

Removal processes get abused — to suppress accurate reporting, to conceal official misconduct, or to burden a small operator with volume. We therefore:

- require specifics, not blanket demands;
- ask what right or law is relied on for legal complaints;
- decline requests whose evident purpose is to suppress legitimate public-interest reporting about public conduct;
- may decline to process repetitive, vexatious or manifestly unfounded requests, and will say so; and
- record and, where appropriate, publish aggregate statistics about requests received and outcomes.

Making a knowingly false statement in a request may itself have legal consequences.

## 8. Transparency

We intend to publish periodic aggregate reporting: how many requests were received, by category, and how they were resolved. Aggregate reporting will not identify requesters or reveal personal information.

## 9. Relationship to Other Policies

This policy is the operative route for complaints raised under the **Copyright and Content Policy**, the **Privacy Policy**, the **POPIA Notice**, the **Source and Attribution Policy**, the **Country and Jurisdiction Data Policy**, and the **Acceptable Use Policy**. Formal records requests follow the **PAIA Manual** instead. Security vulnerabilities follow the **Security Policy**.
