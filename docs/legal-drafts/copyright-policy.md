## 1. Our Position in One Paragraph

InsightWire does not republish other people's articles. For each event we store and display a **headline**, a **short summary or description**, the **name of the source**, the **original source URL**, the **publication timestamp** and our **ingestion timestamp** — and we link back to the original so that the full item is read at its origin, on the publisher's own page, under the publisher's own terms. Copyright in the underlying material stays with its owner. We claim nothing in it.

## 2. What We Store and Display, Precisely

| Element | What it is | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | The item's title, as published | The source feed or API |
| Summary / description | A short description, either the source's own feed description or an automatically generated summary of it | Source feed, or automated summarisation |
| Source name | The publisher or institution | Source configuration |
| Original URL | A link to the item at its origin | The source feed or API |
| Publication timestamp | When the source published it | The source feed or API |
| Ingestion timestamp | When we retrieved it | Our system |
| Extracted entities | People, organisations, places and topics identified in the text | Automated extraction |
| Category and geographic references | Navigational metadata | Automated classification |

**What we do not do:**

- We do not store or display the full text of source articles.
- We do not reproduce articles behind paywalls, and we do not circumvent paywalls, registration walls or access controls.
- We do not present source material as our own work.
- We do not remove, alter or obscure attribution.
- We do not host source imagery, video or audio as a substitute for visiting the source.
- We do not sell, syndicate or licence source material to third parties.

## 3. The Legal Basis We Design To

We design this model around the **Copyright Act 98 of 1978** (South Africa), as amended. The relevant provisions, as they stand at the effective date of this document, are:

**Section 12(1) — fair dealing.** Copyright in a literary work is not infringed by fair dealing for the purposes of research or private study, criticism or review, or **reporting current events**, provided the source is mentioned and, where it appears on the work, the author's name. Reporting current events is precisely what a news-aggregation and research tool supports.

**Section 12(3) — quotation.** Copyright in a literary work lawfully available to the public is not infringed by a quotation from it, **including a quotation in the form of a summary appearing in a newspaper or periodical**, provided the quotation is compatible with fair practice, does not exceed the extent justified by the purpose, and mentions the source and the author's name where it appears on the work. This is the provision closest to what the Platform does: short, attributed, purpose-limited summaries with a link to the original.

**Section 12(8)(a) — no copyright in certain material.** No copyright subsists in official texts of a legislative, administrative or legal nature, or in official translations of such texts, or in speeches of a political nature or speeches delivered in the course of legal proceedings, or in **news of the day that are mere items of press information**. A substantial share of what the Platform ingests — government notices, institutional announcements, official statements, and bare items of press information — sits within this provision.

**Fact is not protected.** Copyright protects the expression of a work, not the underlying facts. That an event occurred, when it occurred, and where, are facts. Our summaries aim to convey the fact and point at the expression, not to substitute for it.

**Status of the Copyright Amendment Bill.** South Africa's Copyright Amendment Bill, which would introduce a broader fair use exception, is not in force at the effective date of this document. Following the Constitutional Court's judgment of 26 June 2026, the Bill cannot be enacted in its current form and requires further legislative attention. **This policy must be re-reviewed if and when the Bill is enacted and commenced**, because a fair use regime would change the analysis.

## 4. How We Keep Ourselves on the Right Side of This

These are operating commitments, not aspirations:

1. **Keep summaries short and non-substitutional.** A summary should make a reader want the original, not replace it. If a summary is long enough that the source article becomes unnecessary, it is too long.
2. **Always attribute.** Source name on every item, on every surface where the item appears.
3. **Always link.** The original URL is preserved and presented as the route to the full item.
4. **Preserve the timestamps.** Publication and ingestion times are part of the attribution and let a reader see how current an item is.
5. **Never circumvent access controls.** We ingest what is publicly offered through a public feed or API, on the terms on which it is offered.
6. **Respect machine-readable signals.** Where a source publishes `robots.txt` directives, feed terms, or API terms of use, we operate within them.
7. **Prefer official and open sources.** Government, institutional and multilateral sources carry the clearest permissions, and much of their output falls under section 12(8)(a).
8. **Honour a rights holder's wishes.** If a publisher asks us to stop ingesting or displaying their material, we will stop, even where we believe we have a legal basis to continue. This is a deliberate policy choice: we would rather lose a source than litigate about one.
9. **Re-check when the law changes.** See section 3 on the Copyright Amendment Bill.

## 5. Automated Summaries and AI Processing

Some summaries and journalist briefs are generated by an automated system (Google's Gemini API, called server-side only). This does not change our copyright position:

- generated summaries are still short, attributed and linked;
- generation is used to condense and describe, not to reproduce;
- source text is sent to the processing provider for the purpose of producing that summary or extracting entities — see the **Privacy Policy** for what is sent and to whom; and
- generated text is not represented as the source's own words. Where a brief is machine-generated, it is presented as such.

**We do not use source material to train, fine-tune or evaluate machine learning models**, and we do not permit others to use Platform content for that purpose without our written permission and regard for the rights of the original sources. See the **Acceptable Use Policy**.

## 6. Ownership

**Source material.** All rights in headlines, article text, images, video, audio and data at the original source belong to the rights holder — the publisher, the author, the institution, the photographer or their licensees. Nothing in the Platform's presentation of that material transfers, licenses or diminishes those rights. Our display of a short attributed summary with a link is not a claim of ownership.

**Our material.** The Platform's software, interface, visual design, database structure, category taxonomy, and the selection and arrangement of the compilation are owned by **[Operator Name]** or its licensors. Where our automatically generated summaries, entity records and metadata attract protection, that protection is ours, subject always to the rights in the underlying source material.

**Your material.** Content you create in the Journalist Workspace — bookmarks, saved searches, timelines and notes — remains yours.

## 7. Your Use of Platform Content

You may:

- read, search and browse the Platform;
- use it for journalism, research, analysis, education and lawful information discovery;
- quote short extracts with attribution to InsightWire **and** to the original source, with a link to the original; and
- follow our links to source material and use it under the source's own terms.

You may not:

- bulk-copy, scrape, harvest or systematically extract Platform content by automated means without our prior written permission;
- republish Platform content in a way that substitutes for the original source, or that strips attribution or the source link;
- present Platform content as your own original reporting;
- resell, syndicate or commercially redistribute Platform content without our prior written permission; or
- use Platform content to train, fine-tune or evaluate machine learning models without our prior written permission.

Where you use Platform content, please attribute both InsightWire and the original source, and link to the original.

## 8. Making a Copyright Complaint

If you own or represent the owner of material you believe is used improperly on the Platform, tell us and we will act promptly. Do not assume you must litigate first.

Send to **[Contact Email]** with the subject line **"Copyright complaint"**:

1. **Your identity and capacity** — your name, organisation, and whether you are the rights holder or authorised to act for them.
2. **The material on the Platform** — the specific item, its URL on the Platform, and the date you saw it.
3. **The material you own** — the URL of the original, and a description of the work.
4. **What is wrong** — for example: the summary is too long and substitutes for the original; attribution is missing or incorrect; the source link is wrong or absent; the material is behind a paywall and should not appear at all; you have withdrawn permission to ingest this feed.
5. **What you want** — correction of attribution, shortening or replacement of the summary, removal of the item, or removal of the source from ingestion entirely.
6. **A statement** that you have a good-faith belief the use is not authorised, and that the information you have given is accurate.

**What happens next.** Copyright complaints enter the workflow in the **Removal and Correction Request Policy** under the "copyright complaint" category: acknowledgement, review, and then restriction, removal, correction or restoration, with a written outcome to you. Where a complaint is clear on its face — for example a request to stop ingesting a feed — we will normally act first and discuss afterwards. Where a complaint is contested, we may restrict the item while we assess it.

**A note on ECTA.** Chapter XI of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002 provides limitations of liability for certain service providers, together with a take-down notification procedure in section 77. Section 72 makes those limitations available **only to a service provider that is a member of a recognised industry representative body and has adopted and implemented that body's official code of conduct**. **[Operator Name] should not assume the benefit of Chapter XI unless it has actually joined such a body** — in South African practice, typically the Internet Service Providers' Association. Whether to join, and whether the Platform qualifies as a "service provider" for these purposes, is a decision for counsel. In the meantime we operate the complaint process above voluntarily and in good faith, and we will accept a section 77 take-down notification if one is sent.

## 9. Counter-Notice and Restoration

If we restrict or remove material and you believe we were wrong, write to **[Contact Email]** explaining why. We will re-review. Where we conclude we acted incorrectly, we will restore the material and record why. We would rather correct a wrong removal than leave it standing.

## 10. Repeat Infringement and Source Withdrawal

Where a source repeatedly supplies material that we cannot lawfully display, or where a rights holder withdraws permission, we will remove that source from ingestion. Removing a source does not automatically delete its historical items; historical handling follows the **Data Retention Policy** and any specific request made under the **Removal and Correction Request Policy**.
